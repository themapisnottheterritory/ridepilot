# Customers: finding, adding, fare category, addresses

**Find a customer**: click **Customers** in the top menu and search by name. Click the name to open the record.

**Add a customer**: on the Customers page use the new-customer button, fill in the **Details** panel (name, phone, date of birth if known), add the home address, and save. RidePilot warns if it thinks the person already exists.

**Edit a customer**: open the customer, click **Edit** at the top, change what you need, then click **Update** at the top.

**Fare category (important for fares)**: on the Edit page, in the **Details** panel just under **Elderly?**, set **Fare category** to Adult, Senior 60+, Disabled, Youth 5-17 or Youth 0-5, then click **Update**. If it is left "Not set", RidePilot quotes the Adult fare on every trip for that rider (unless the trip itself is marked senior or disabled under Number of Passengers Tracking). Set it once for each regular rider.

**A customer's trips**: the customer's page has a **Trips** panel (top right) with their next 10 trips under **Coming up** and their last 10 under **Recent**: date, time, from and to, and whether each is on a run, not on a run yet, or how it ended. Click a date to open the trip. (On the trip form, **Trip Summary** beside the customer's name also lists their past or future trips and lets you double-click one to copy it.)

**Hover a customer's name** anywhere in RidePilot (the Customers and Trips lists, Dispatch) and rest the pointer on it for a moment: a small card shows their phone, home address, mobility needs, fare category, funding source, any alert on their record, and their next and last trip, so you can tell two riders with the same name apart without opening the record.

**Addresses and map pins**: each address needs a correct map pin, because distances, fares, the driver's navigation and the Route Optimizer all use it. Some Goliad and Lavaca addresses imported in September were placed at the centre of their town (Yoakum, Fannin, Victoria) because the street was not on the map. If a pickup or drop-off shows in the wrong spot or a trip's miles look wrong, edit that address, check the location on the map, move the pin to the house, and save.

**Eligibility, mobility, funding**: the Eligibility, Mobility Configuration and Funding Authorization Numbers panels on the customer record hold ADA eligibility, wheelchair/mobility needs and funding details. Mobility matters for seats and wheelchair spaces on the bus.

**Where an address belongs**: a rider's **home** goes on the rider's record: open the rider, **Addresses** panel, **Add**, pick the address from the suggestions, and tick **Mailing** on it. Only that rider sees it. **Saved places** (the **Addresses** page) are for places many riders go: clinics, dialysis, stores, employers, offices. Everyone who books sees them, so never save a rider's home there, never name a place after a person, and give each place its real name, not just its street. RidePilot asks "This looks like someone's home" if a new saved place is called Home, is named after a rider, has only a street address for a name, or is a rider's home address; and it says "Not on the map yet" when a rider's address has no map pin. If the house number isn't in the suggestions, type just the street and town in the search box, pick the street, then type the house number in the **Address** field below the search box. Or click **Save** anyway: when the address isn't on the map, RidePilot offers **Place it on the map**, which looks up the house (on our own map, then with the US Census address lookup, sending only the street address) and, if neither knows the house, puts the pin on the street; the message says which, so check the pin. A printable guide: http://10.0.0.16/address-guide.pdf

**Pins to check (where drivers stop)**: every night RidePilot looks at the bus GPS for each finished pickup and drop-off and notes where the van sat still. Once drivers have stopped at the same spot on three or more different days, that is where drivers stop for that address. If it is far from the address's map pin, the address appears on **Pins to check** (link on the **Addresses** page). Look at both points on the map, then click **Use where drivers stop** to move the pin there, or **Keep the pin**. Nothing moves until someone chooses, and a move can be undone from the same page for a week. Only buses with a working GPS unit count, so county vans don't add stops yet.
