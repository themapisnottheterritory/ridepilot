Decisions so far

  • Account-based ledger in RidePilot, token-agnostic (section 4). Every option shares this part.
  • Frequency: 13.56 MHz, decided 2026-09-10. Philz and Andrew both concur (section 2). MIFARE / NTAG cards, the ESP32 + RC522 prototype, and 13.56 MHz USB HID readers. The 125 kHz EH301 and its EM4100 cards are out.
  • Pilot two tokens on one backend: RFID card on one bus, QR code on another (section 8).
  • Phase 1 built 2026-09-10 (section 11): migration, ledger, office pages, activity report.
  • Phase 2 built 2026-09-10 (section 12): tap endpoint, tablet scanner, offline queue, QR sheets, fare settings. QR codes are read by a USB 2D barcode scanner, not the tablet camera, so both tokens share one path.
  • Phase 3 built 2026-09-10 (section 13): demand-response pickup tap, online reload job (Stripe pull, not yet configured).
  • Tap to pay with bank card or phone wallet explored and paused (section 10). Percentage fees do not fit a $1.50 fare.
  • Connectivity is not the constraint. Every bus has a Pepwave MAX BR1 LTE router and the tablets have their own LTE. Offline is a fallback, not the design center.
  • Fare policy settled 2026-09-10 (section 17): published fixed-route fares, the distance-band rural table, ADA paratransit $1.50 inside Victoria, 60+ everywhere, 10-ride 10% off, 20-ride 20% off, monthly unlimited $30 or $15 reduced, transfers 90 minutes onto a different route once per paid fare, balance may go to -$5.00. All set in production.

2Core decisions

Account-based, not card-based

The card carries nothing but its factory UID. The balance lives in RidePilot on the customer, not on the card.

  • No writers, ever. Every cheap reader can read a UID.
  • No keys, no encryption, no personalisation step. Issuing a card is "scan it, pick the customer".
  • Lost card: flip the row to blocked, hand out a new one, balance follows the customer.
  • Several active cards per customer is fine, for a rider and a caregiver, say.
  • The token does not have to be a card. A printed QR code, or later a bank-card reference, hangs off the same customer and ledger. Section 4 models this as fare_tokens with a kind.

Frequency: 13.56 MHz, decided

The two prototypes use different frequencies and their cards are not interchangeable.

Decided 2026-09-10: 13.56 MHz. Andrew concurs. The ESP32 + RC522 prototype is already on the right frequency. The EH301 form factor, an enclosed mounted reader, is still useful at a desk or a lobby station if you buy a 13.56 MHz variant. The 125 kHz unit you have becomes a bench tool.

Whatever the reader, it must emit the UID in one canonical format. Standardise on uppercase hex, for example 04A3B2C1D9E6F0, and convert on the way in if a reader insists on decimal. Store the canonical form once and look up by it.

No Pi on the bus, at least to start

The driver tablets already run the RideAVL app and talk to the driver API. A USB HID reader on an OTG cable behaves as a keyboard and types the UID into whatever field has focus. The driver app adds a hidden, always-focused capture field and posts the UID.

  • One cable and one reader per bus. No second device to power, network, mount or patch.
  • Run context, driver identity and GPS all come free from the tablet.
  • Connectivity: Pepwave MAX BR1 on every bus plus LTE in the tablet. Taps go straight to the server. The offline queue only covers dead spots and router reboots.
  • The ESP32 + RC522 build becomes the rider-facing validator later. An ESP32-S2 or S3 has native USB and can act as a HID keyboard itself, beep, light green or red, and still just type the UID into the tablet. The tablet stays the only thing that talks to the server.

A Pi only earns its place as a standalone station (section 6), never as bus-side middleware.

5Tap flow

Fixed route, the easy one

One tap is one boarding. The driver app is on the walk-on screen. An RFID reader types the UID, a QR is read by the tablet camera, and both land on the same endpoint.

POST /api/v1/runs/:id/token_taps
{ uid, client_uuid, recorded_at, latitude, longitude, stop_id? }
  1. Look up the token by uid. Unknown, lost or blocked: 404 or 422, tablet shows red, driver falls back to the normal cash and pass buttons.
  2. Resolve customer, then default rider category, then fare from the fixed-route lookup amounts already in fare_types.fare_factor and the route default fare added 2026-09-04.
  3. Transfer check, decided 2026-09-10: a paid tap by this rider inside the 90-minute window, on a different route, with no transfer already used on that fare, means fare 0 and the boarding is recorded as Free / Transfer. Chaining Red to Pink to Red pays on the way home.
  4. Pass check: a valid pass means fare 0.
  5. Write one FixedRouteBoarding (boarded_count 1, fare type Card, fare_amount) and one ledger debit in a transaction. Return the new balance so the tablet can flash it.
  6. Below the negative floor: 422 with the balance. Tablet shows red plus the balance, driver takes cash.

Offline, as a fallback only given the Pepwave and tablet LTE. At run start the tablet fetches uid, customer name, rider category, balance and pass status for all active tokens, a few hundred rows. Taps are accepted locally and queued with a client_uuid, same as boardings today. On sync the server recomputes. A tap that ends up under the floor is recorded anyway and the account is flagged for the office.

UDR / demand response, a little more complex

The trip already knows the customer and the fare, and has fare_collected_time. A tap at pickup is a confirmation and a debit, not a lookup.

POST /api/v1/trips/:id/token_tap
{ uid, client_uuid, recorded_at }
  1. Resolve token to customer.
  2. If it is the trip's customer: debit the trip fare, set fare_collected_time, set the trip fare to a payment fare. This reuses the logic already in update_fare.
  3. If the card belongs to someone else: 409 with both names. Tablet asks "Charge Elmer Street's card for Thalia Acosta's trip?" Yes debits that customer and records both ids. This covers caregivers paying for riders.
  4. Guests and attendants are a policy decision. Simplest rule: one tap covers the whole trip fare as RidePilot already computes it. Per-person taps can come later.
  5. Below floor: same as fixed route. Never strand anyone. Flag the account.

Offline works the same way because the trip fare is already on the tablet.

6Recharge, cheapest first

  1. Front desk in RidePilot. A staff page: scan the card with the same USB reader on the PC, or type the printed serial. See customer and balance, enter amount, pick cash or check, print a receipt. One reader. This is the whole MVP.
  2. Driver reload on the bus. Same ledger. Driver enters the amount and takes cash, reconciled against the run's cash total in the existing driver run reports. Common in rural systems, but it puts cash in drivers' hands. Your call.
  3. Online. Stripe or Square payment links, no monthly fee. A scheduled job inside the network pulls completed payments and posts load transactions. Nothing inbound, which keeps to the house rule that internal apps never face the internet. The rider identifies the account with the printed card serial. Card-not-present fees are 2.9% + $0.30, so a $10 load costs $0.59 (5.9%) and a $20 load $0.88 (4.4%). Nudge riders toward $20 loads if it matters.
  4. Balance-check station. Pi Zero 2 W, reader, small screen, in the lobby. Shows balance and the last five rides. Cheap, riders like it, no cash inside so no security problem.
  5. Cash kiosk: skip it. The bill validator and a vandal-resistant enclosure cost more than the rest of this project combined, and the front desk already takes cash.

7Risks accepted

  • UID cloning. A phone can clone a UID-only card. At a couple of dollars a ride, with a ledger that makes duplicate patterns visible, this is not worth engineering against. If it ever is, NTAG and MIFARE secure sectors work on the same cards and readers with a firmware change on the ESP32 validator.
  • Reader output drift. Decimal versus hex, with or without leading zeros. Solved by normalising to hex on the server and buying one reader model.
  • Negative balances. Bounded by the floor and visible on a report.
  • Tablet port wear. The OTG cable stays plugged in. Strain-relieve it.

10Tap to pay: explored and paused

"Tap to pay" here means the rider taps a bank card or phone wallet. Two ways to do it were looked at on 2026-09-10 and both are parked. The ledger in section 4 is built so either can be added later without touching the tables.

Why it is paused: the numbers at a $1.50 fare

Percentage-plus-fixed fees are designed for coffee, not for a fare we are trying to push below $1.50.

ChannelFee on one $1.50 fareShare of fareNet to agency

Scaled to 10,000 fares, which is $15,000 gross:

Three things fall out of that table:

  1. Per-fare card processing costs an eighth to a sixth of revenue. That is the same money a fare cut would give riders. It goes to the processor instead.
  2. The fixed cents hurt more than the percentage. The $0.15 alone is 10% of a $1.50 fare. Charging a bank card once per $10 or $20 load instead of once per ride brings the fee down to 3 to 4%.
  3. Closed loop is free per tap and the whole hardware bill is under $300. The trade is front-desk time taking cash and checks, which we already spend today.

The other reasons

  • Riders. A rural, older, disabled and lower-income ridership carries fewer bank cards and smartphones. A token we issue at the desk works for everyone. A bank card does not.
  • Transit logic. Stripe and Square terminals know nothing about transfers, reduced fares or passes. All of that would still be built in RidePilot, so tap to pay saves no engineering. It only changes the token.
  • Flow. A merchant terminal makes the driver start a charge for an amount before the rider taps. That is slower at the door than a reader that just reads.

If it comes back

The cheap re-entry is Stripe Tap to Pay on the tablet as a reload channel: the driver taps a rider's bank card once to add $10 or $20 to their account, then the rider uses their RFID card or QR for each ride. It removes cash from the bus, costs one fee per load, and needs no validator. In the schema it is one fare_transactions load row with payment method card_online and the Stripe id in the reference field. The bank_card_ref token kind exists for the day a rider wants the bank card itself to be their token, at which point the per-tap fee argument gets re-run at whatever the fare is then.

11Phase 1 as built

Commit "Fare cards phase 1", 2026-09-10. All the September work was done on a branch called fixed-route-wp8, which was fast-forwarded into master and deleted on 2026-09-11, so every commit named on this page is on master. Everything here is office-side. Nothing touches the tablet yet.

Deploy

docker exec ridepilot_app_1 sh -c 'cd /var/www/ridepilot && bin/rails db:migrate && bin/rake ridepilot:add_v2_custom_reports'

The migration drops the old fare_cards and fare_card_data tables, six test rows, and the pre-commit hook comment was updated to match.

Code

  • app/services/fare_ledger.rb is the only thing that changes a balance: load, debit, refund, adjust, transfer. Row lock on the customer, floor check on debits with an override for a tap that already happened offline, idempotent on client_uuid. Phase 2's tap endpoint calls debit.
  • FareToken lookup resolves what a reader typed: separators stripped, upcased, then the other radix with the usual zero padding, so an office reader set to decimal still finds a card the bus reader reads in hex. Serial lookup accepts #55 or 55.
  • QR tokens mint their own 12-character uid, no vowels, never starting with a digit. Printing the QR is phase 2.
  • A posted ledger row is read-only. Corrections are further rows.

Pages

  • Fare Cards in the top nav. The scan box holds focus so a USB HID reader goes straight to the rider. Today's loads by method, balances outstanding, every rider with a token or a balance.
  • Rider fare account, also a button on the rider record. Balance and pass, tokens with Lost, Block, Reactivate and Retire, an issue form that takes the UID from the reader plus the printed serial, a load form with $5, $10 and $20 buttons and cash or check, refund and adjustment behind a disclosure with a required reason, a paged ledger, and an optional printable receipt.
  • Fare Card Activity under Reports. Date range, grouped by day, payment method, type, posted by, or rider. Cash in, checks in, fares, refunds, adjustments, net, and the total riders hold on account.

Permissions. Editors at a scheduling provider can manage tokens and postings. Read-only roles see the pages without the forms.

Left for phase 2. Driver API tap endpoints, tablet capture field, offline snapshot, QR printing, an office UI for the pass product and the rider category. The columns for those last two already exist.

12Phase 2 as built

RidePilot commit "Fare cards phase 2 (server)". Tablet rideavl-v2 version 1.0.8, commit "Fare card taps on the fixed-route screen". The APK sits at ~/ridepilot-ops/rideavl-1.0.8-fare-cards.apk and has not yet been copied to the pilot download slot. That copy is the deploy step.

One decision changed from the plan. QR is read by a USB 2D barcode scanner, not the tablet camera. A $25 keyboard-wedge scanner types the QR's text exactly the way the RFID reader types a UID, so both tokens share one code path on the tablet, and nothing needs the camera, a Capacitor plugin or a permission prompt. Camera scanning can still come later if a bus wants it.

Deploy

docker exec ridepilot_app_1 sh -c 'cd /var/www/ridepilot && bundle install && bin/rails db:migrate'
# tablets: install rideavl-1.0.8-fare-cards.apk, or copy it to public/rideavl-pilot.apk and commit

The bundle install is for rqrcode. Gems live in the bundle_cache volume, so no image rebuild.

Server

  • Tap endpoint. POST /api/v1/runs/:id/token_taps takes the uid, a client UUID, the time, the nearest stop and the GPS fix. It answers with the usual boardings payload plus the rider's name, category, fare type, fare, balance, and pass, transfer, double-tap and duplicate flags. Failures carry a code: unknown token with the normalised uid, token not usable, below floor with balance and fare, or tap failed.
  • Offline snapshot. GET /api/v1/runs/:id/fare_tokens lists every active token with rider name, category fare, balance, floor and pass status. The tablet caches it per run.
  • Rules live in one service, FareTap, exactly as section 5.1 says. Unknown, blocked or inactive is refused. A second tap on the same run inside two minutes is ignored. A valid pass records the boarding under the Pass fare type at no charge. An earlier tap inside the provider's transfer window records it under Free / Transfer at no charge. Otherwise the rider category's default fare times the Card factor is debited through the ledger. Below the floor is refused unless the tap already happened offline. A printed serial works in place of a uid. Phase 3's trip tap reuses the same resolver.
  • Undo refunds. Undoing a tapped walk-on from the tablet puts the fare back, idempotently.
  • Boardings gained customer and token columns, so reports can say who rode. The walk-on JSON carries the rider's name and a tapped flag.
  • Office. Rider category, pass expiry and a per-rider floor are editable on the fare account page. Each QR token has a card-sized print sheet.

Tablet, rideavl-v2 1.0.8

  • Keyboard wedge. A document-level key listener treats a burst of keys under 80 ms apart, at least four long and ending in Enter, as a scan. Keys typed into a real input are ignored, and the soft keyboard never appears because nothing holds focus.
  • Tap flow. The scan posts to the tap endpoint, or queues in IndexedDB and replays flagged offline. A green card shows name, category, fare and balance for four seconds, with pass, transfer and already-tapped variants. A red card shows unknown, refused, or balance too low with "take cash" for seven seconds. While offline the cached snapshot greets the rider by name and estimates the balance.
  • Fallback. A toolbar card button lets the driver type the printed number when the reader is down. Tapped walk-ons show the rider's name in Recent.

13Phase 3 as built

RidePilot commit "Fare cards phase 3 (server)". Tablet rideavl-v2 version 1.0.9 at ~/ridepilot-ops/rideavl-1.0.9-fare-cards.apk, superseding 1.0.8 and still not copied to the pilot download slot.

Deploy

docker exec ridepilot_app_1 sh -c 'cd /var/www/ridepilot && bundle install && bin/rails db:migrate'

Then set the demand-response card fare on the provider page under Fare related settings. Three fields are new there: the card fare per demand-response trip, the lowest balance allowed, and the transfer window. Until the card fare is set, a tap at pickup uses the amount on the trip, and failing that the rider's category fare.

Demand-response pickup tap

  • Endpoint. POST /api/v1/trips/:id/token_tap with the uid, a client UUID, and optionally the amount the driver typed and a mismatch confirmation. The trip must be on one of the driver's runs. Amount precedence is what the driver typed, then the amount on the trip, then the provider's card fare, then the rider's category fare.
  • Someone else's card answers 409 with both names. The tablet asks "Charge X's card for Y's trip?" and resends confirmed. The ledger row notes who it paid for.
  • One tap covers the whole trip, guests and attendants included. A valid pass is free but still marks the fare collected. Free and donation trips refuse a tap, as does an already collected one.
  • Undo refunds. Deleting the tap, or undoing the pickup itself on the tablet, refunds and clears the collected mark, so the ledger never disagrees with the trip.
  • Pickup screen. The fare box prefills, a badge says "has a card" with the balance when a tap is expected, and afterwards the trip shows "paid by card" with an undo. Card payments at pickup are online only, like every other manifest action. Offline the tablet says take cash.

Online reloads

  • A rake task, fare_cards:sync_online_reloads, pulls paid Stripe Checkout Sessions from the last seven days and posts one online load per session, idempotent on the session id. Nothing inbound. It runs from cron inside the network.
  • The rider is matched by the card number they type into the Payment Link's custom field, which is the printed serial. A number nobody has is listed as unmatched in the job output for the office to load by hand from the receipt email.
  • Not yet configured. There is no Stripe account or key. To turn it on: create the account and a Payment Link with a required text field labelled "Fare card number", put the secret key in the app environment, add a half-hourly cron line next to the nightly scheduler, and print the link's QR on the card sheet and receipt. Fee reminder from section 10: nudge riders to $20 loads.

Specs. 61 fare examples across the ledger, tokens, both tap flows, the driver API, the office pages, the report and the Stripe sync with a stubbed client, all green.

Everything in the plan is now built except hardware. What remains is the pilot itself: order the readers and cards from section 12, issue cards at the desk, install 1.0.9 on the two pilot buses, and watch the Fare Card Activity report for a month.

16Pass sales as built

Answers the mechanics of question 6. The prices are still the team's call. Live in production with both prices at their defaults, so no discount and no monthly button until a price is set.

  • 10-ride and 20-ride are stored value. The card is credited rides times the rider's category fare, so a senior's 10-ride is $5.00 of value and the tap takes $0.50 a ride. Each pass has its own discount. The card still gets the full value and the office takes less cash. The ledger records the cash actually taken separately, and the activity report's cash and check totals come from that.
  • Monthly is the expiry date on the customer. Selling one loads the pass price and debits it again in one transaction, so the money is on the ledger, the balance is unchanged, and the report shows pass revenue in its own column. The form offers this month or next, defaulting to next from the 24th, and expiry is the last day of that month. Both taps already honour it.
  • Office. A "Sell a pass" panel on the rider's fare account page with the three buttons priced for that rider, paid by cash or check, with an optional receipt that shows the pass and what was paid. The two prices live on the provider fare page.

The Victoria Transit fare card

No more exact change. Load money on a card at the transit office, then tap the card on the reader as you board. The fare comes off your balance and the driver's screen shows what is left.

Getting a card. Bring a photo ID to the transit office at [ADDRESS]. Tell us your rider category (senior, disability, youth) and we will set it on the card so you always pay the right fare. Cards are free. Lost your card? Tell us and we will block it and give you a new one. Your balance moves to the new card.

Loading money. At the transit office with cash or check. Any amount. You get a printed receipt with your balance.

Rides and passes. Buy ten or twenty rides at once and save, or ride all you want for a month.

AdultYouth 5 to 17Senior 60+ / Disability
Single ride$1.00$0.75$0.50
10 rides, 10% off$9.00$6.75$4.50
20 rides, 20% off$16.00$12.00$8.00
Monthly pass, unlimited rides$30.00$15.00$15.00

The monthly pass is good through the last day of the month on every Victoria Transit bus and on rural demand-response trips in Victoria and DeWitt counties.

Transfers. Changing buses to finish one trip is free. Tap your card on the second bus within 90 minutes of paying on the first. The transfer is good for a different route only, once per paid fare. Riding the same route back is a new fare.

Using the card on demand-response and paratransit trips. Tap the card when the driver picks you up. The trip fare comes off your balance.

Checking your balance. The driver's screen shows it every time you tap, and it is on every receipt. Or call the office.

Running low. If your balance cannot cover a fare, the driver will tell you. You can pay cash for that ride and reload at the office.

Plan your trip

Not sure which bus to take? Use the trip planner at plan.gcrpc.org. Enter where you are and where you are going, and it shows which route to ride, where to board, and when the bus comes. It works on your phone and can use your location. Fixed routes only. Call 361-578-8775 for a demand-response or paratransit ride.

Questions: 361-578-8775, Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.
